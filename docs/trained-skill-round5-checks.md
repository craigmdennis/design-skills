# conversation-prose: run these on the draft before sending

Procedures, not word lists. A word list finds only the words on it. Run every check on every sentence and on every clause, including transitions, lead-ins, subordinate clauses, participial phrases, and trailing clauses.

Check the framing sentences last and separately: the opener, the closer, every transition, every lead-in. A sentence that reports no finding is the one a proof-read skips, and it produced the past failures.

Count the words in each sentence before anything else. Past 25, split it at the coordinating conjunction, coordinated sentences included. The sentence you rewrote or merged during a check is the one that crosses 25.

A quoted phrase under discussion is a mention and passes every check; the checks judge your own wording. After shortening a sentence or merging two, re-run check 16: compression is where a pronoun gains two referents.

1. **Animacy.** Every verb, including ones in subordinate and participial clauses. Non-human subject? Test the verb. Any verb you would not apply to a rock (wants, knows, wins, beats, earns, refuses, deserves, argues, claims) gets rewritten: name the person who acted, or use is/has/contains/produces/stops/removes/states/fails/applies. A document does not argue: "a second summary that argues your position" → "that states your position". The closing offer is where this verb appears most often.
2. **Literal restatement.** Does each word name what physically happened? If it names one thing to mean another, answer who did what to what and write that. No milder word inside the same image. An abstract noun given a physical property or motion fails too: "share one shape", "the construction moved", "the strongest test coverage" (give the number of cases). An abstract noun as the subject of an action verb fails as well ("a restart of the API pods ended the failures"): name who acted. A release, a run, and a pass over the data are the same shape ("The August 16 release added a 60-second time to live", "the weekly sitemap pass re-lists every URL"): name who added it, name the crawler. "passed" fails with a file, a test, or a run as the subject ("`0142_backfill.sql` passed on staging" → "the staging run of `0142_backfill.sql` completed with no errors"). "against" and "versus" mean compared with: write two facts ("Downtime was 4 minutes. The announced downtime was 30 minutes."). An adjective can give code a property of a living thing ("timezone-sensitive" → "produces different results in different timezones"). "is" between a statement and an operation fails ("`select * from orders where user_id = $1` is a sequential scan" → "Postgres does a sequential scan for each `select * from orders where user_id = $1`"). Ordering gets stated as ordering ("the session store comes first" → "the session store must be built before the SAML work starts"). Developer idiom fails as well: shipped → released, happy path → the flow with no error cases, raised an objection → stated it, blocker → the thing that stops the work, I own the ticket → I am responsible for the ticket, support escalated → support reported the failures, rollback → a return to the earlier version, boot → every time the service starts, dry-run mode → a mode that reports the matched rows and deletes nothing, spike ticket → the ticket covering the investigation, window → the announced downtime, spin up → start, test coverage → the tests, covers every case → tests every case, pass over the data → run, lose the rows → delete the rows. Only a technical name in backticks is exempt, and it exempts only itself ("a warning about `utf8mb3` on every boot" keeps `utf8mb3` and replaces boot). A number, a state, and a span of time do not move: "the price rose to $35" → "someone changed the Pro plan price to $35 in the database", "the locks cleared" → "the database released the locks when the UPDATE stopped", "2 hours have passed on the converted 24 call sites" → "I have spent about 2 hours on the converted 24 call sites". A component is not a duration: "the session store is about 2 weeks" → "building the session store takes about 2 weeks".
3. **Two-word verbs.** Try the plain one-word verb. If it means the same thing, the phrase was figurative. Use the plain verb.
4. **First sentence.** It states the finding. Not context, not the question restated, not agreement.
5. **Permission narration.** Delete "I decided", "I went ahead", "rather than asking", "I took the liberty". Give the call itself.
6. **Justification clauses.** Delete any clause explaining why your choice was better, in any position.
7. **Self-evaluation.** Delete any word rating your own work: earned, paid off, shines, elegant, clean, nice. Blame counts too: "the failure was mine", "my mistake", "I got that wrong".
8. **Signposting and significance.** Take each sentence's subject. If it names a part of the message (the cause, the point, the reason, the version, one thing) and the sentence only describes what comes next, delete it: "The mechanical cause is narrower", "Worth stating plainly". Delete any sentence or clause explaining why a finding matters, including a trailing "which is exactly why...". Delete any "worth ...ing" with a speech act, wherever it sits: worth naming, worth stating, worth flagging, worth noting, worth mentioning. Any position. A count of the message's own items is the same subject, whatever the verb: "One follow-up remains", "Two things are left". The count fails as an object as well: "I found four problems in the nine changed files", above a list of the four. A subject that points at adjacent visible content ('the draft below') and only lists what it states gets deleted too. A sentence reporting an omission or a scope call stays.
9. **Repeated meaning.** One concept, one word. List every object, action, and decision the draft names, then compare every mention of each. The synonym pairs share no word, so no search finds them: "the decision"/"the final call", "the production backup"/"last night's snapshot". A verb names a concept too, so one event takes one verb: "completed"/"finished", "updated"/"changed", "rejected 91 attempts"/"ended the failures". A description standing in for a name is a second name as well: "`src/scheduler/`"/"a directory with no tests", "a restore from that production backup"/"a restore of production". A category word is the common second name: "that module" for "`lib/http/retry.ts`", "the diff" for "the nine changed files", "the process" for "the service", "the production run" for "the production UPDATE", "the Pro plan price" for "the database price", "the change" for "copied the 12 lines into `workers/publish.js`", "the recorded outcome" for "Priya decided to migrate the notification service to SQS in Q4". The storage word and the domain word name one set: "31,406 rows"/"every draft", "a server-side record"/"server-side sessions". Two words for one concept can stand inside one sentence: "batch backfills in chunks of 10,000 rows" → "in batches of 10,000 rows". The closer is where a description replaces the name: the body names "the fix", the closer writes "the `config/db.yml` change", so keep "the fix". A state and a quantity take one verb as well as an event: "logs a warning every time the service starts"/"is still present", "requires a 54 MB bit array"/"stores about 60 bytes per URL"/"would take roughly 1.8 GB". Reuse the first term and the first verb in every mention, the closing sentence included. Check 16 adds a qualifier to the first noun and keeps that noun ("the production UPDATE" stays "UPDATE"). A second name for one object also fails 16.
10. **Subject position.** Read the first five words of each sentence. Fix fronted clauses that switch subject, two stacked clauses or clefts, "there is/are" with a nameable actor, and every mid-sentence appositive.
11. **Negated contrast.** Delete any "X, not Y" you wrote, in any word order, across a comma, a dash, or a full stop. Every negator counts, including the ones carrying no "not": no, none, nothing, no one ("Nothing was added to scope — the work was discovered"). State the positive claim.
12. **Banned list.** Check the reader's banned words, in every form each entry names. Never clip a multi-word term to its head noun, in any position: list each multi-word term, find every mention, write it in full each time. Three positions produce the clipping — the second clause of a coordinated sentence ("the checkout error rate" → "the rate"), a sentence opener several sentences later ("the Bloom filter" → "The filter hashes each URL"), and a new paragraph after the full term appeared in the paragraph above ("the self-hosted RabbitMQ cluster" → "The cluster", "the notification service" → "The service"). Every mention takes the full term, including each mention that follows a full one. A demonstrative in place of the qualifier is the fourth position ("a contract appendix" → "that appendix", "the `src/api/validate.js` copy" → "that copy"): a demonstrative is not a qualifier. A full mention elsewhere in the same sentence does not permit the clipping ("`src/routes/invites.js` line 52" in full beside "line 87").
13. **Structure.** No headers or tables on a short answer. Three or more steps or conditions take a numbered list.
14. **Question answered with work.** A why is answered with a why.
15. **Noun clusters.** Three nouns maximum in one stack. Four or more ("design system adoption evaluation framework") gets rewritten with a preposition or a relative clause.
16. **One reading.** Take each pronoun and each definite noun phrase. Two possible referents in the preceding text means naming the thing. Candidates include actions, hypothetical mentions, and members of a named set. A singular after a plural takes the member's name ('the contract appendix' after three contracts). A plural after a set and its subset takes the count ('the four pods', 'all six pods'). Two variants of one operation or one number: name the variant in every mention ('the production run', 'the existing tests', 'the August 4 estimate of 2 weeks'). A coordinated sentence keeping the full term in the first clause and the head noun or a pronoun in the second gets the full term in both ('the checkout error rate peaked at 46%', 'and the 5.7 server is decommissioned on Friday'), even where the sentence reads longer for it. An attribute of the named thing takes the name too ('I am responsible for NOTIF-812 and the due date is September 5' → 'and the NOTIF-812 due date is September 5'). An HTTP method and a request that uses it are two candidates ('the same POST' → 'the same POST request to the orders API'). A sentence opening on 'That' or 'This' points at a whole preceding clause: write the noun ('The false positive rate is acceptable because...'). A demonstrative with a noun is the same shape ('that change', 'that copy'): write the member's name. A general class and one specific instance are two candidates ('the Bloom filter' after the one in `internal/dedupe/filter.go` and a Bloom filter in general → 'this Bloom filter' for the specific one). A pronoun after a request and the service it targets takes the noun ('it' → 'the POST request to the orders API'). The closing question or offer fails this check most often.
