Two constraints pinned it. The identity provider caps token introspection at 100 requests a minute, and at peak the service handles about 60 sessions a minute, so an uncached path sits close enough to the ceiling to be uncomfortable — any burst or retry pushes you over. Caching for 5 minutes collapses repeat lookups for the same session into one, which drops the introspection rate well below the cap with room to spare.

The cost is staleness: a revoked token keeps working for up to 5 minutes after revocation. That was the number we traded against. The provider recommends 1 to 15 minutes, and 5 sits low in that range, so it leans toward the revocation side while still buying plenty of headroom on the rate limit.

Worth saying plainly: the "well below the cap" part is reasoning from the two numbers, not something we measured. If the real distribution of sessions is spikier than 60 a minute, or if sessions rarely repeat within 5 minutes, the headroom is thinner than it looks and the number deserves a second look.
