It was a tradeoff between the provider's rate limit and how long a revoked token stays usable.

The limit is 100 introspections per minute, and we peak around 60 sessions per minute. Uncached, that's already 60% of the budget in steady state, with nothing left for bursts or retries. Caching for 5 minutes means each token is introspected once per window instead of once per request, which drops the steady-state rate well under the ceiling and leaves real headroom.

The cost is that a revoked token can keep working for up to 5 minutes. That's why we didn't go higher: the provider recommends 1–15 minutes, and 5 sits at the low end of that range, so we get most of the rate reduction while keeping the revocation window short. Going to 15 would barely improve the request rate but would triple the exposure window.

If revocation latency turns out to matter more than we assumed, the lever is to lower this toward 1–2 minutes and check the resulting request rate against the 100/min limit at peak.
