# Bulk Export for the Reporting Dashboard

**Status:** Approved for first release

## Problem

The reporting dashboard exports one report at a time. Ten reports require ten separate exports. A hundred reports require a hundred separate exports. The repetition is tedious enough that customers submit support tickets about it, and a ticket asking for bulk export arrives every week.

The requests are not evenly spread. They come from accounts holding more than 50 reports. That group is about 8 per cent of accounts and about 40 per cent of revenue. A small share of customers therefore submits a steady, recurring complaint, and that share is the one whose loss costs the most revenue.

## Solution

A user selects multiple reports and exports them in one action.

A large export takes time, so the export does not run in the browser. It runs as a background job. When the job finishes, the user receives an email containing a download link. The link expires 24 hours after it is issued.

## Requirements

**Background execution.** The export runs as a background job. The user does not have to keep the dashboard open or wait for the job to complete.

**Email notification.** On completion, the system emails the user a link to the finished export.

**Link expiry.** Download links expire 24 hours after they are issued.

**Format.** The first release supports CSV only.

**Job size limit.** A job covering more than 500 reports is rejected. The rejection message states the limit, so that the user knows the maximum instead of guessing it.

## Out of scope for the first release

Parquet and JSON output. Both are planned for a later release. Neither is a prerequisite for this release.

## Success measure

The weekly count of bulk-export support tickets. Success is that count reaching zero within two months of release.

This is a direct measure rather than a proxy: the tickets are the problem, and their absence is the required outcome. If tickets continue after two months, the feature has not covered the cases customers actually have. The remaining tickets are then read before the next piece of work is chosen. The likeliest candidates are the deferred formats or a higher job limit.
