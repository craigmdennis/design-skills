The decision was a tradeoff between the provider's rate limit and how long a revoked token stays usable.

The limit is 100 introspections per minute, and we peak around 60 sessions per minute. Without the cache, each request needs one introspection, so the steady-state rate is 60% of the limit, with nothing left for bursts or retries. With a 5-minute cache, each token needs one introspection per window. That brings the steady-state rate well under 100 per minute and leaves spare capacity.

The cost is that a revoked token stays usable for up to 5 minutes. The provider recommends 1–15 minutes. The 5-minute value is at the low end of that range, so it gives most of the rate reduction and keeps the revocation time short. A 15-minute cache would improve the request rate very little and would triple the time a revoked token stays usable.

If revocation latency is more important than we assumed, lower the cache time toward 1–2 minutes and check the resulting request rate against the 100/min limit at peak.
