We traded the provider's rate limit against the time a revoked token stays usable.

The limit is 100 introspections per minute, and our peak is about 60 sessions per minute. Without caching, 60 introspections per minute is 60% of the limit in steady state, and that leaves nothing for bursts or retries. With a 5-minute cache, each token is introspected once per window. Without the cache, each request causes an introspection. The steady-state rate is then well below the limit, and capacity remains for bursts and retries.

The cost is that a revoked token is still usable for up to 5 minutes. The provider recommends 1–15 minutes, and 5 minutes is at the low end of that range. We get most of the rate reduction, and the time a revoked token stays usable is short. A 15-minute cache would improve the request rate only slightly, and it would triple the time a revoked token stays usable.

If revocation latency matters more than we assumed, lower the cache time toward 1–2 minutes and check the resulting request rate against the 100/min limit at peak.
